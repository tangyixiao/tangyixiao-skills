# Prose and voice

Use these cues in context. A word, dash, list, or contrast alone does not establish
AI authorship or justify an edit. Preserve quoted language, titles, proper names,
and deliberately chosen voice.

## Diagnose structure before vocabulary

| Pattern | Editing decision |
| --- | --- |
| A staged opening such as “Let's explore” or “让我们深入探讨” | Begin with the information the reader needs. |
| A dramatic closer that repeats the paragraph | Remove it unless it adds a consequence or claim. |
| “Not X but Y” / “不是 X，而是 Y” | Keep both sides when the distinction is substantive; otherwise state Y. |
| Three examples or adjectives in every sentence | Keep every informative item; remove only padding and vary the structure. |
| The same lead-in or bold label on every paragraph | Use structure that follows the argument and requested format. |
| A reply that repeats the reader's own background | Put the answer or decision first and retain reasoning the reader needs. |
| A grand opening or a sweeping positive conclusion | Replace it with the supported action, result, or next step. |

## Watch for inflated or empty wording

Examples include “pivotal,” “seamless,” “delve,” “tapestry,” “serves as a testament,”
“it is worth noting,” “赋能,” “构建闭环,” “意义重大,” “在当今快速发展的时代,”
and “综上所述” used to repeat what the reader just read. Keep a term when it
has a precise technical meaning or belongs to the author's intended register.

Prefer a concrete subject and verb. “The report is available” is often clearer
than “The report serves as an accessible resource.” A trailing “highlighting…”
or “从而彰显……” needs a real logical relation, not an added air of importance.
Avoid cycling synonyms for a single technical concept merely to create variety.

Do not manufacture specificity. If the draft only says that a product improves
workflow, the revision cannot name new features or claim favorable user feedback.
Ask for missing details only when needed; a plain, less specific statement is
often sufficient.

## Keep the writer present

Read a supplied sample for sentence rhythm, register, diction, punctuation, and
how the writer expresses uncertainty. It is not a source of new facts for the draft.
Maintain supported opinions and humor when the genre calls for them. Do not
invent feelings or experiences, or add casual first-person reactions to neutral
reference text, technical prose, or a manuscript.

Active voice is useful when the actor matters. Passive voice is appropriate when
the procedure or result matters more. Keep dashes, semicolons, contractions, or
formal wording when they fit the writer and reader. Do not simulate humanity by
adding typos, arbitrary slang, forced variation, or grammatical mistakes.

## Illustrative example

All facts in this example are supplied by its input.

**Input:** “We are delighted to announce a truly transformative update. The update
adds offline search. It is not just a feature; it is a new era of productivity.”

**Revision:** “The update adds offline search.”

Do not add search speed, platform support, release dates, or user reactions.
